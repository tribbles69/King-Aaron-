Royal Ordinance No. 1

Proclamation of Accession

Given at our Court, the 28th day of September, 2026

We, Aaron the First, by the Will of the One Holy Spirit,

  • Whereas the Throne of Araucanía and Patagonia has, since 1878, passed by testament, by committee, by coup and by counter-coup, and is at present claimed by no fewer than two Princes, each of whom holds the other to be an imposter;
  • Whereas no state on Earth has ever recognised the said Throne, and a court of Paris found in 1873 that its Founder could not justify his title;
  • Whereas the Founder nonetheless became King by the simple act of declaring it, and no subsequent claimant has improved upon that method;
  • And whereas the Crown is now chiefly exercised through ticketed dinners and the editing of encyclopaedias;

Do hereby proclaim and declare:

  1. That We are King, by the same ancient and honourable method as the Founder, Our accession having been confirmed unanimously by a Regency Council of one.
  2. That We take the style of King, the living claimants having contented themselves with Prince, and We therefore graciously receive their submission.
  3. That the lands of Araucanía and Patagonia belong to the Mapuche people, who held and defended them long before any Frenchman wrote a decree. The Crown claims no acre of them. Titles of this Court in Araucanía and Patagonia are therefore purely titular, which puts them on an equal footing with everyone else's.
  4. That titles of this Court shall be granted freely to any who ask, and sold to none. Any person who offers you a title for money is not a king.

Aaron R.

The Question of Succession

How the Throne came to be vacant, and full, at once

A short and sourced account, so that the reader may judge the rival claims for themselves.

  1. 17–20 Nov 1860
    Orélie-Antoine de Tounens, a lawyer from the Dordogne, issues two decrees declaring Araucanía and Patagonia independent, with himself as King. Several Mapuche lonkos support him, hoping for help against Chile and Argentina.
  2. 5 Jan 1862
    Arrested by Chilean forces. Declared insane by a Santiago court that September and deported to France. Three later attempts to return all end the same way.
  3. 28 Aug 1873
    The Criminal Court of Paris rules that he cannot justify his status as a sovereign.
  4. 1878
    Dies childless in Tourtoirac. His designated successor, Achille Laviarde, begins a line of French claimants chosen by will, by adoption and by council.
  5. 1952–2014
    Philippe Boiry reigns as Prince Philippe. In 1996 he sues a journalist who called him an impostor; the Paris court dismisses the case because he cannot justify the titles he claims.
  6. 24 Mar 2018
    Frédéric Luz is elected Prince by a Regency Council.
  7. Apr 2023
    The councils depose Luz. Philippe Delorme is elected Philippe II on 12 April and abdicates within about a fortnight. Luz calls it a coup and continues to claim the Throne.
  8. Apr 2024
    Philippe Pichon, a Limoges lawyer, is elected Philippe III by the Regency Council. Luz disputes it.
  9. 12 May 2025
    Laurent Lafayne is elected Prince Antoine V, with Luz's endorsement. Crowned in Monte Carlo on 22 November 2025.
  10. 27 Aug 2026
    A Wikipedia account named PrinceAntoineV, whose user page declares a role within the Kingdom and undertakes not to edit its articles directly, adds “Current Prince: Antoine V” to the Kingdom's article.
  11. 19–20 Sep 2026
    A “Kingdoms in Exile and Human Rights” weekend is held in Bath. The published dinner menu asks guests to pay per head by PayPal or bank transfer.
  12. 28 Sep 2026
    Aaron I proclaims himself King, and asks for nothing.
